Three VaR numbers, because the disagreement is the information

A VaR figure without its method attached is not interpretable. Historical, Gaussian and Cornish-Fisher VaR disagree on the same portfolio, and how much they disagree tells you how far the return distribution is from the one the parametric number assumes.

So the report gives all three and flags the gap. A service that quoted one number would be easier to read and would hide the only thing worth knowing about it.

Cornish-Fisher was reported below Gaussian until a sign was fixed

On a fat left tail the Cornish-Fisher correction must push VaR up. The first version applied the expansion to the positive quantile and negated afterwards, which flips the sign of the skew term and produced a corrected VaR below the uncorrected one — the exact opposite of the correction's purpose. The formula looked right; printing the two numbers side by side made it obvious in a second.

The last column is the point of the service

The HIDDEN-RISK portfolio looks well diversified by capital — eight positions, 7.8 effective positions, nothing above 18%. By risk it is not: the top position carries 1.53× its capital share.

Contributions come from an Euler decomposition and sum exactly to portfolio volatility, which is what makes them an allocation of risk rather than a set of scores. A capital-based position limit is structurally unable to see this, which is why the ratio is a check rather than a chart.

Every rejection names what is wrong and what was received. The alignment check matters most: a ticker with no market data is refused rather than dropped, because silent misalignment is what makes a risk system dangerous rather than merely wrong — the numbers still come out, they just describe a different portfolio.

Reproduce it

cd risk-portfolio-saas
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m pytest tests/ -q
python -m src.demo

The service surface is one function — handle(request, market), JSON in and JSON out — so a FastAPI or Flask layer is a thin adapter rather than a rewrite. None is included, because the framework is not the part worth showing.

What this does not establish

  • Synthetic market data. No real prices, no real portfolio, no client.
  • 750 observations per name. A 95% historical VaR estimated from this much data has a wide confidence interval that the report does not show.
  • Single-period risk only. No liquidity horizon, no margin, no funding, and no netting across accounts.
  • Stress scenarios are single-factor beta shocks. Real stress testing requires correlated multi-factor scenarios and a view on how correlations themselves move.
  • No persistence, authentication, tenancy or rate limiting — none of the things that make a service a service. This is the analytics core only.
